# Where Is My Stuff?

A lost-and-found site for Karachi. You post something you lost or something you found, and the app looks for a match nearby. A wallet left in Saddar, a bag found at a bus stop in Gulshan-e-Iqbal, that sort of thing.

It isn't public yet. For now it only runs on my own machine.

## How it works

1. Pick Lost or Found and fill in the report. It takes five steps: Type, Details, Location, Matching, Review.
2. Say where it happened. Type a place or landmark, then pin it on the map, use your phone's location, or enter the coordinates yourself.
3. Answer a few private questions about the item. They change with the category, and they're what separates your black wallet from every other black wallet.
4. Look through the matches. The app compares reports on category, distance, date, description, and your private answers.
5. Arrange the return. Propose a meeting place and time. Once the other person accepts, you both see each other's contact details. At or after the meeting time, either of you can mark the item as returned.

One account can post both lost and found reports. The app never checks who really owns something. A high match or a QR scan proves nothing, so the two of you sort that out when you meet.

## What's in it

You can report wallets, cards and IDs, keys, electronics, bags, documents, cash, and a catch-all Other. Wallet questions change with what's inside. A wallet is still one report whether it's empty or holds six cards. The bank and payment options are HBL, MCB, UBL, Meezan Bank, NayaPay, SadaPay, JazzCash, Easypaisa, and Other.

Place names and handover spots are free text, since nobody agrees on how to write a Karachi address anyway.

Two reports only match if they come from different people, one is lost and the other found, both are still open, and they're in the same category. They also need at least two meaningful details in common.

Drafts save when you press Next, so you can leave halfway and pick up later. Nothing saves while you type.

Photos are optional, up to 5 MB. The app strips location data from them and shrinks big ones.

There are two kinds of QR code. A report QR lets a finder sign in and start a Found report from a Lost report's public details. A personal QR label is a printable sticker for your own stuff. It stays active for six months, and you can switch it off and on again whenever you like.

You can propose, accept, dismiss, and restore handovers, and record the return when it happens. Your history has search, filters, and readable report IDs. Staff can close or remove reports, and every action is logged.

## Privacy

Your private answers are only compared behind the scenes. They never show up on the match or management pages. Public descriptions get screened for personal details like phone numbers.

Photos are visible to the report's owner and the people in a match, nobody else.

Contact details on a report appear only after both sides agree to a handover. Personal QR labels work differently. While a label is active, anyone who scans it sees your phone number. That's the whole point of the label. Once it expires or you turn it off, the number is hidden.

Some questionnaires ask for full identifiers, and those answers are stored as given, without extra encryption. Keep that in mind before you type in anything you wouldn't want sitting in a database.

## Where things stand

Reports, matching, handovers, the dashboard, QR labels, and photos all work.

Not done yet: password reset, profile editing, notifications, in-app help, and a public launch. Going public also needs a proper security review first.
